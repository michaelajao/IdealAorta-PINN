All 66 tests pass on this machine (the README says 58 of 66). The weaknesses are in the gates:
- Capacity checks are too narrow. They cover only the noisy (σ = 0.02) conditions on one draw. They never cover the noise-free conditions, which include the whole near-wall versus random-removal control.
- The convergence threshold is too loose to certify the 2% gates. It is latent, because the queued pilot uses the direct solver.
- The boundary gate is weak. It is the RBFs' own selection criterion, so they can't fail it. It also averages over all wall nodes, so it misses slip in the aneurysm zone where WSS is scored.
Design.
- The paper's comparator is missing. The pilot omits the paper's prespecified RBF, the space-time RBF and a naive "voxel average treated as a point value" baseline. So it can't show whether the paper's ranking survives noise and averaging.
- The pressure outcome is partly built in. The new RBFs fit each snapshot separately. Under independent noise, their two-snapshot acceleration is structurally worse than the space-time neural field's.
- Tuning stops at the edge of the grid. Both RBFs picked the largest radius (20 mm) and smallest ridge (1e-9) tried, and the queued pilot reuses that grid.
- Partial-volume averaging at the wall is never modelled. Volume averaging uses only boxes entirely inside the lumen (Case 1: 3,037 of 5,970 locations), yet near-wall voxels matter most for WSS.
